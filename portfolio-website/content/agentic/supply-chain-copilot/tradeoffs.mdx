## What we chose, and what it cost

Every architectural decision has a counter-position. The notes below
are the ones I expect to be challenged in review.

### pgvector over a managed vector DB

Postgres-native retrieval keeps contracts, vectors, and audit log in
one transactional store. We give up best-in-class recall@k on very
large corpora — we never expect >5M chunks per tenant — and gain a
single backup/restore story for compliance.

### Two agents, not one

A planner+critic split costs ~280ms and ~30% more tokens than a
single-shot prompt. It bought a 6× drop in hallucinated SKUs and made
the system reviewable by the buyer team. Worth the spend at this
dollar-volume.

### Human-in-the-loop, always

Auto-approve was scoped out for v1. The agent is allowed to surface a
recommendation, never to commit a purchase order. Reduces blast
radius to zero at the cost of full automation.

### What I'd revisit

- Caching: lead-time queries repeat heavily — a 60s TTL would shave
  ~22% of tool calls.
- Eval surface: 240 scenarios is too narrow; needs a synthetic
  generator.
- Critic latency: a 7B distilled critic would likely match Opus at
  1/4 the time.
